Settings that change how a person can reach the robot.

6a Runtime Settings — every control here changes the operator's physical ability to use the panel, so each one has a live preview beside it rather than a number you have to imagine. 6b the maintenance sheet: what the 1.5-second hold actually opens, drawn over the screen it interrupts.

Neither screen carries STOP. Both are reached through the maintenance hold, which means the robot is already held — and a STOP on a screen where nothing can move teaches people that STOP is decoration. Change anything in 6a and watch the preview; hold the ⋯ in 6b's bar.

These are the UserProfile fields that already exist — font_scale, motor_accessibility_preset, dwell_ms, scan_period_ms, repeat_guard_ms, deadzone, audio_cues — plus language. Milliseconds are meaningless to the person they affect, so the rail shows a real control behaving the way the current settings will make it behave.

Preview, not a number

A dwell of 1000 ms means nothing until you hold a button for it. The rail's control is the real one — dwell, repeat guard, text size and language all apply to it as you change them.

Irrelevant settings dim

Dwell time only exists for the dwell preset; scan period only for scan. The other cards go to the §09 interlocked treatment with a word, rather than accepting input that will do nothing.

Not here

Topics, frames, publish rate and speed limits. A settings screen an operator can reach must not be able to change what the app sends.

6b Maintenance sheet what the 1.5-second hold opens

Hold the ⋯ in the bar. The hold is drawn as a fill on the button itself, the robot is held at zeros the moment the sheet opens, and the Drive screen stays visible behind it — an operator needs to see that the thing they were doing is still there. Release before 1.5 s and nothing happens.

Closing resumes publishing immediately. Nothing here changes what the app sends — topics, frame and rate are shown so you can read them, not set them.
The hold is visible

The fill is on the ⋯ button itself, in pollen — a held state that ends by itself. Today it looks like a menu, which is why people tap it and nothing happens.

Held, not stopped

Opening the sheet holds the robot at zeros and says so in the bar and the sheet. That is different from STOP, which latches — so it uses pollen, not error.

Read, don't set

Six facts an engineer asks for over the phone — link, rate, frame, profile, device class, app version — as values, not fields. Switching role needs its own hold.
