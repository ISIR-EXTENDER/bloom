# Design review — Drive, both robots

**Date** 2026-09-17 · **Screens** `manager_drive` (explorer-manager, kinova-manager) ·
**Reference** the Joystick Lab UX review · **Outcome** Drive split into Bench and Operator layouts

Format: finding → decision → where it landed.

---

## F1 · The gripper card is cut

`drive-gripper` was authored 130×130 with `show_details: true`. `ToggleWidget` renders three stacked
rows — title, 56 px button, then the literal topic `/gripper_controller/commands`. It needs 250×144
and has 130×130, inside `overflow: hidden`.

**Decision.** Resize, and turn details off: an operator cannot act on a topic name. The label also
became a verb — *Close gripper*, not *Closed* — and the commanded state moved to the card header,
where it is a fact rather than a promise. A button says what it does; a header says what is true.

**Landed.** `drive-gripper` 590,14 300×120 (bench) and 1054,14 212×168 (operator), details off,
`offLabel`/`onLabel` now verbs. `screens/drive.md`.

## F2 · Pivot is shorter than Height

`drive-rz` was 130×260 next to `drive-z` at 130×440. Two controls of the same kind, on the same row,
at different sizes — and 260 is below the vertical slider's own minimum, so the readout clipped too.

**Decision.** Not "make Pivot 440". Group controls by **axis type** rather than by shape: Height
vertical with the Translation pad (linear), Pivot horizontal under Rotation (angular). This is why
the Joystick Lab screen reads well, and it makes the asymmetry meaningful instead of accidental.

Height stays vertical in both layouts. A draft that made it horizontal to match Pivot tested badly
in review: a sideways drag for a vertical motion is exactly the translation tax the product removes.

**Landed.** Bench `drive-rz` 588,558 400×104 horizontal; operator 742,546 300×116 horizontal.
Rule 3 (sibling symmetry) added to `README.md`.

## F3 · Speed readouts cut mid-digit

`drive-max-linear-speed` was 210×130 rendering title + unit + a `min → max` range on one header
line. At 210 px the range ran past the card edge.

**Decision.** 300×132 is the horizontal slider minimum with details on. On Drive they go to 364×120
with details off (bench) — and on the operator layout they stop being sliders at all.

**Landed.** `DELTAS.md`, `widget-min-size.md`.

## F4 · The top row cannot hold its six cards

With details on, the widgets Drive declares need 250 + 300 + 300 + 3×170 plus five 12 px gutters =
1420 px in a 1252 px row. Turning details off is not a preference here; it is what makes the row fit.

**Decision.** `show_details` is a geometry setting, not a cosmetic one, and it is per widget.
Runtime default becomes `false`; bench keeps it on for the pads and Height only.

**Landed.** Defaults table in `design-system.html` §08.

## F5 · kinova's fault echo is 120×60

`drive-fault` is a `topic-echo` at 120×60. The minimum is 226×200 — a twist is eight lines of mono.
Drive on kinova is over-subscribed by two widgets.

**Decision.** The echo is diagnostic: move it to Robot feedback at 336×380. `drive-fault-reset`
stays on Drive, correctly sized, because clearing a fault is an operating action. Drive geometry is
then identical on both robots, which also satisfies invariant 06.

**Landed.** `seed/kinova-manager.json`; the three feedback echoes reflow to 288 wide.

## F6 · Three non-token colours

`#4a9eff` (translation axis) is the only pure blue in the product. `#e0685f` (rotation axis) is close
enough to `error` to read as a warning on a control that is perfectly safe. `#7fa95f` is a hard-coded
joystick fallback in `control-renderers.tsx`.

**Decision.** Translation → `#7e967e` sage, rotation → `#c98a7e` clay: same lightness, different hue,
distinguishable without either reading as an alarm. The fallback becomes `--bloom-color-sage`.

**Landed.** Both seed files (all screens). The `control-renderers.tsx` fallback is a code change in
the PR.

*Amended 2026-09-24:* `control-renderers.tsx` was split; the joystick's default colour is now the
`--bloom-axis-translation` token in `JoystickPrimitive.tsx`.

## F7 · STOP looks like a label

A white card with red text, the same visual weight as every widget around it, and movable by an app
author. At a glance it is a label, not a control.

**Decision.** STOP is chrome: filled error, full-bleed in the status rail, same corner on every
screen of every app, minimum 176×120, exempt from fit-scaling below 64 px of glass. Press stops
instantly; resuming is a one-second hold drawn as a fill. Stopped dims the pads to 45% and makes them
inert while mode and frame buttons stay live, so the operator can prepare the resume.

**Landed.** `design-system.html` §10. Seeds now declare a `reserved_regions` entry the runtime must
honour instead of an author placing a widget.

## F8 · The kiosk bar is missing the screen name and the publish rate

The two things that tell an operator which screen they are on and that the tablet is still alive.
The hamburger also reads as a menu, when maintenance is a 1.5 s hold.

**Decision.** Six things in the bar and nothing else: app, screen, truthful link status, command
frame, publish rate, profile, maintenance hold. The hold is drawn as a fill. The profile slot now
carries the role, because a supervisor walking up must be able to read it without touching anything.

**Landed.** `design-system.html` §10.

## F9 · Two audiences, one screen

Bench and operator needs conflict on exactly one axis — whether the screen reports what it publishes.

**Decision.** Two layouts in one app, selected by `UserProfile.preferred_control_layout_id`, which
already exists in `backend/libs/config/models.py`, is typed in the API client, asserted in
`test_config_models.py`, populated in `explorer-user-tests.json` — and read by no frontend file.
Named *Bench*, not *Developer* or *Advanced*: it says where you are standing rather than who is
cleverer, and it does not invite an operator to go looking for the advanced one.

**Landed.** ADR 0133, three profiles per app, `screens/drive.md`.

## F10 · What the two roles actually differ on

**Decision (Susi).** Keep Bench and Joystick Lab separate, and reframe the Bench/Operator split:
Operator is the **accessible** layout for people with motor disabilities and differs in *language*
as much as geometry (Slow / Medium / Fast, not 0.15 m/s; verbs, not topics; 64 px targets). Bench is
the **debugging** layout, mostly used by people without motor impairment, so more information and
smaller targets — 48 px density, detail strips where geometry allows — are legitimate there.

Consequence for ADR 0133: a role varies **wording** as well as layout, target size and density.
The list of things it may never vary is unchanged.

**Landed.** `screens/drive.md`, `screens/joystick-lab.md`, option `3c`.
